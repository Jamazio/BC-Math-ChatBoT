Institutional Overview: 
Benedict College is a private, co-educational, liberal arts historically black college located in Columbia, South Carolina. Founded on December 12, 1870, the institution operates under the inspiring motto, "A Power for Good in Society." Today, Benedict is led by Dr. Roslyn Clark Artis, who serves as the fourteenth president and the first female chief executive officer in the college's long history. The campus spans approximately 110 acres near downtown Columbia, anchoring the historic Waverly community. As a member of the NCAA Division II and the Southern Intercollegiate Athletic Conference, the college is well known for its athletic programs and its vibrant student body of roughly 2,100 scholars. 

Historical Foundations: 
The historical roots of Benedict College extend deep into the American Reconstruction era. The institution was established under the auspices of the American Baptist Home Mission Society following a generous philanthropic gift. Bathsheba A. Benedict of Pawtucket, Rhode Island, donated thirteen thousand dollars from her late husband’s estate to purchase a 110-acre former slave plantation. The original Benedict Institute sought to provide education and paths to self-sufficiency for newly emancipated African Americans. Because antebellum laws had strictly prohibited teaching enslaved people to read, early classes focused on basic grammar, reading, writing, and theology. In 1894, the South Carolina State Legislature officially chartered the institution as a four-year liberal arts college, renaming it Benedict 	College. For its first sixty years, the college was led by white northern Baptist ministers until 1930, when Reverend John J. Starks became the first African American alumnus to assume the presidency. 

Campus Infrastructure: 
The physical campus seamlessly blends historic preservation with modern institutional infrastructure. A significant portion of the grounds is recognized as the Benedict College Historic District and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Among these treasured landmarks is Duckett Hall, a multi-story red-brick building constructed in 1925 that features classical columns and symmetrical architecture. This historic facility currently anchors the college's business school. In contrast to these historic spaces, the David H. Swinton Campus Center offers a contemporary glass-and-brick design that serves as the central hub for student dining, recreation, and campus activities. Off-campus athletic events and major community celebrations are held at the nearby Charlie W. Johnson Stadium, which accommodates eleven thousand spectators. 

Academic Structure and Distinctions: 
Institutionally accredited by the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges, Benedict College offers twenty-six baccalaureate degrees and two master's programs. The academic architecture is organized into specialized schools that prepare students for competitive, modern careers. The Tyrone Adam Burroughs School of Business and Entrepreneurship holds national accreditation from the Accreditation Council for Business Schools and Programs and offers specialized paths in accounting, finance, and supply chain management alongside a Master of Business Administration. 

The School of Science and Engineering features a highly acclaimed Environmental Engineering program, making Benedict the first HBCU in South Carolina and only the second nationwide to achieve prestigious ABET accreditation for this discipline. Furthermore, the School of Education, Health, and Human Services boasts a social work program accredited by the Council on Social Work Education, as well as cutting-edge majors in public health and esports administration. Media and arts education thrive within the School of Communication, Art, and Social Sciences, which offers degrees in mass communication, cybersecurity, and criminal justice. 

Leadership and Unique Demographics: 
Under the modern leadership of Dr. Roslyn Clark Artis, Benedict College has achieved historic academic milestones while completely defying national enrollment trends. While many higher education institutions across the United States face severe enrollment declines, particularly among male undergraduates, Benedict has established a unique demographic profile. Nationwide, male enrollment at historically black colleges hovers around thirty-seven percent, but Benedict completely reverses this trend by maintaining a student body that is approximately fifty-four percent male. The institution is widely studied by educational researchers as a premier national model for successfully recruiting, retaining, and graduating young Black male scholars. 

Social Mobility and Economic Impact: 
Benedict College has gained significant national recognition for its progressive approach to affordability and economic empowerment. In a historic move designed to alleviate the burden of student loan debt, the college executed an aggressive twenty-six percent reduction in its standard tuition costs. This dedication to access has earned Benedict top marks from publications like Washington Monthly, which consistently ranks the institution as a national leader in social mobility for its success in graduating low-income students and launching them into lucrative careers. Beyond its impact on individual families, Benedict serves as a massive economic engine for the region. Studies commissioned by the United Negro College Fund reveal that Benedict College generates an annual economic footprint of over one hundred thirty million dollars, directly supports more than one thousand two hundred local jobs, and significantly boosts the lifetime earnings of its graduates. 

Student Life and Traditions: 
Student life at Benedict College is rich with tradition, community engagement, and cultural pride. The campus supports an active Greek life community through the National Pan-Hellenic Council, alongside numerous academic honor societies and student organizations. A cornerstone of the college's cultural legacy is the annual Harambee Festival, which has grown into one of the largest college-sponsored African American cultural festivals in the nation, drawing thousands of visitors to celebrate art, music, and community health initiatives. On the athletic front, the Benedict Tigers field fourteen varsity sports teams. The football program has achieved remarkable regional success, clinching back-to-back conference championships, while the famous Marching Tiger Band of Distinction remains a celebrated campus icon known for its high-energy performances and showstopping choreography. 

Mission Statement: 
Benedict College is committed to providing transformative learning experiences characterized by high quality academic, co-curricular and extra-curricular programming, intentionally designed to develop superior cultural and professional competencies for a diverse student body. 

A Vision for Benedict College: 
Benedict College (BC) aspires to be a leader in providing transformative learning experiences for a diverse student body; defined by superior cultural and professional competencies that are nurtured and developed by faculty, staff, and stakeholders who value innovation, customer service, community, and industry engagement.   
Benedict College is an institution where each student’s experience in academic, residential, and co-curricular activities should be transformative, and customer-service focused. Benedict’s faculty and staff work in a collaborative, transparent environment where data­-based decisions are grounded by empathy and compassion.   
Benedict College is a place of full-time learning — from the classroom to the playing field, to residence halls and into the community — where the legacy of “BC” as inclusive, pioneering, and “family-oriented” is respected and cherished.  
Inclusivity, innovation in technology and enterprise, transformative educational experiences, and commitment to community all merge in a dynamic environment where students receive an education that prepares them to enter the global marketplace with exceptional cultural and professional competencies that empower them to contribute to the sustainability and economic growth of the communities where they live, work, and play.   
Benedict College is not only a power for good in society, but Benedict is committed to providing a transformative learning environment, created by highly trained and culturally competent professionals, that empowers all students to realize their highest potential.  

 
 
At a Glance: 
Founded in 1870 by a woman, Bathsheba A. Benedict, Benedict College is a private co-educational liberal arts institution with over 1,700 students enrolled in its 26 baccalaureate degree programs and 2 masters degree programs. Defying trends, Benedict College maintains a nearly equal male and female student population. This Midlands HBCU welcomes students from all 46 counties in South Carolina, 30 states across America, and 26 countries around the world. 
Benedict offers several high-demand fields of study in STEM, Cyber Security, Mass Communication, Sport Management, Business Administration, Engineering, Computer Science, Biology, and Education. The College also has a diverse faculty of which 80 percent are full-time, and 71 percent hold doctorates or the equivalent. 
There are over 18,000 proud Benedict Tigers throughout the nation. Benedict College has been a community leader for over 152-years and is a significant contributor to South Carolina and the region. Contributing $130 million and 1,218 jobs in total local and annual economic impact, a Benedict graduate working full-time throughout his or her working life can expect to earn $1.1 million in additional income because of their Benedict College degree. 
The College made front-page news in the spring of 2018 when it became the first South Carolina college to lower its tuition by 26 percent. Cutting tuition drew praise from the Commission on Higher Education, South Carolina’s education oversight body. The commissioner noted that the move Benedict College made should be applauded because it offers families affordability and students greater access to higher education. 
Benedict College has been highly regarded and exceptionally ranked for its programs by several academic and traditional publications. For example, Benedict College was ranked as one of the top baccalaureate colleges in the nation by Washington Monthly magazine for creating social mobility, and producing cutting-edge scholarship and research. In 2019, Benedict College received the 2019 ACE/ Fidelity Investments Awards for Institutional Transformation and was named the HBCU of the Year by HBCU Digest. Learn more about the HBCU legacy.  
Benedict College is accredited by the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges to award baccalaureate and masters degrees. Five of the College’s degree programs hold national accreditation: The School of Education, Social Work, Environmental Health Science, Arts and Sciences and the Tyrone Adam Burroughs School of Business and Entrepreneurship.

Background Information:
Benedict College is a private historically black college in Columbia, South Carolina, United States. Founded in 1870 by northern Baptists, it was originally a teachers' college. It has since expanded to offer majors in many disciplines across the liberal arts. The campus includes buildings in the Benedict College Historic District, a historic area listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
Benedict College was founded in 1870 on the land of a former 110-acre (45 ha) plantation in Columbia, South Carolina. Representing the American Baptist Home Mission Society, Bathsheba A. Benedict of Pawtucket, Rhode Island had provided $13,000 to purchase the property. This was one of the numerous educational institutions founded in the South for formerly enslaved people by northern religious mission societies, as education was seen as key to the future for African Americans. Benedict Institute opened on December 12, 1870.

Accreditations:
The Tyrone Adam Burroughs School of Business and Entrepreneurship is accredited by the Accreditation Council of Business Schools and Programs (ACBSP).
The Educator Preparation programs are accredited by the Council for the Accreditation of Educator Preparation (CAEP) through 2022.
Benedict College Social Work Program was the 1st HBCU social work program in South Carolina to be nationally accredited by the Council on Social Work Education (CSWE).
Benedict College is accredited by the National Association of Art and Design (NASAD).
Benedict College is the 2nd HBCU in the nation and the 1st in South Carolina to receive ABET accreditation for its Environmental Engineering Program.
Benedict College’s Environmental Health Science program is accredited by the Environmental Health Accreditation Council (EHAC).
Benedict College is accredited by Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges to award baccalaureate degrees.
